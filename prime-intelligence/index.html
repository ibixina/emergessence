01 / THE PERSON

The person.

I’m Shishir. I study how intelligent behavior emerges from computation.

My work sits between neuroscience, artificial intelligence, and cognitive psychology. I use computational models and behavioral experiments to explore perception, learning, and the representations a mind builds of the world.

Questions I keep returning to

  • How does a fragment bring back a memory?
  • How does a mind build a world that isn’t there?
  • How does an environment shape intelligent behavior?

Ways of working

Dynamical systems. Information theory. Neural networks. Models that have to meet behavior somewhere.

A memory may be less like an address, more like a neighborhood.

I investigate hierarchical representations in artificial neural networks and their correspondence to biological neural systems. Associative memory offers one way to ask how a part can lead us back to a whole.

An internal world, without new sensory input.

Mental imagery and simulation expose the models a brain constructs from experience. I investigate the role of prediction errors in learning, and how these internal models relate to sensory input.

The questions

  • What changes when a model’s prediction fails?
  • Which structure survives in a compact representation?
  • What does an internally generated experience borrow from perception?

The atlas’s folded structure is a conceptual illustration of internal models, not a measured embedding.

I build computational models of bottom-up and top-down influences on information selection. Intelligence lives in an interaction: a model acts, the world responds, and the next observation changes.

Organization without an organizer

Here, 180 individuals follow three local rules: separate, align, and stay near neighbors. Move your pointer through the flock, or scatter it.

The slider scales alignment and cohesion. Separation prevents collisions; your pointer repels nearby individuals.
